# Yearly Letters



{% hint style="success" %}
This experiment is complete. 11 years later, I continue writing myself a letter each year. Usually, in the fall or winter.
{% endhint %}

Since 2012, I’ve been writing a letter to myself each year. Over these 15 letters, the practice/container has remained fairly stable.

I write the letter in the fall/winter, somewhere I feel open, grounded, and away from the routines of a typical week. Historically, this has meant being in the forest, by a body of water, or in a library.

I write the letter in a single sitting and rarely for more than two hours. I arrive free from an  expectation of what I might write. What I do arrive with is clarity about who I’m speaking to (myself) and what “the act” is (listening). In this way, the writing is improvisation.&#x20;

To my surprise, I remarked on this point in the 2019 → 2020 letter:

> I like that this practice has turned into not requiring much preparation. It’s a recording of what i can recall on a given day. In this way, it feels ‘truer’ and/or closer to how I generally am. I am not affected by having revisited a bunch of pictures or journal entries from this past year. I am feeling into myself and affording space for…the things I practice (do, think, feel) everyday to come to the foreground.

I see both Pings and these yearly letters as artifacts of personal attunement. What distinguishes them is their scale. Pings arrive to me as words, phrases, sometimes sentences, and always by surprise. They’re swift fleeting specks. I don’t know what they might meanor how (if at all) I might use them. The yearly letters are longer, both in length (often exceeding a single-spaced page) and in the window I capture them in (\~1 hour compared to seconds for a Ping). As a result, these letters take on more structure as I relate where I am, where I’m coming from, and where I might explore next.

The two also support one another. While writing the 2025 → 2026 letter, two Pings emerged that ended up providing me enduring direction and grounding this past year. I wonder whether this will happen again when I write this year’s letter (which I’ve not yet done!)
